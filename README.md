🌕ABOUT THE PROJECT🌑

Humanity’s exploration on Mars and the Moon have left more than just footprints and flags. In search of life and further discoveries, humanity has left more than a few spacecrafts on the surface of these heavenly bodies. Some of these crafts have functioned while seeing the daylight for more than a few years, while others have been left abandoned due to heavy loads and/or system malfunctions. Some of these have completed their missions, while others got no chance to.

Yet, each of these spacecrafts represents a new era of humanity. A new era of scientific ambition. A new era of engineering and development on technology– and most importantly, new discoveries. 

Our project, Abandoned But Not Forgotten brings these artifacts back into the spotlight. Instead of presenting these space equipments as technical facts, we present them with an immersive educational storytelling experience in the form of an interactive platform. Our goal is simple: to show everyone that every piece of equipment left behind has a story worth telling.




🎯THE PROBLEMS WE ARE ADDRESSING🎯

Space exploration is often resembled and remembered with the successful launches, historic discoveries, photographs and groundbreaking discoveries. However, the equipment that made this possible is left behind and in turn gets way less attention. Therefore, many people are unaware of:

1. What spacecrafts remain on the surface of the Moon and Mars.
2. Why these objects were sent there.
3. Why they mattered (and why they still matter today).
4. What their mission was.
5. Where they are located.
6. What the scientific discoveries were that they helped us make.

We wanted to make this information available to everyone of interest and wished to make it engaging, as well as immersive. And that, we did.

For the solution, we built a digital museum where users can explore the history of the artifacts left on the Moon and Mars. The experience includes the use of 3D models, visual storytelling and educational facts. Rather than plainly stating the truth about each artifact, the museum tells the user about each artifact’s purpose, its history, and its contribution to space exploration.




⭐KEY FEATURES⭐

Planetary Atlas: Explore the artifacts left on the Moon and Mars through visual representation and 3D modeling.
Interactive Exhibit gallery: Browse a collection of abandoned space equipment and explore their backstories in depth.
Interactive 3D Models: View highlighted and/or selected space equipment through interactive 3D models.
Mars & Moon Exploration: Explore the Moon and Mars with us as we take you on a wonderful storytelling journey through the artifacts.
Mission Timeline: View the timeline of each launch, each mission and each discovery made in a flash.
Compare View: Explore and compare between two selected artifacts to understand their differences and purposes.
Responsive Experience: Our website is developed to provide you with a great storytelling documentative experience on both desktop and handheld devices.








🛠️TECHNOLOGY USED🛠️

The project uses web technologies and 3D based rendering to provide a wonderful collection on our interactive digital museum.

- React — Component-based user interface.
- TypeScript — Structured and maintainable application logic.
- Three.js — Rendering and displaying interactive 3D content.
- GLB / glTF — 3D model asset format.
- HTML5 and CSS3 — Web structure, styling, and layout.
- JavaScript — Interactive functionality.
- Git and GitHub — Version control and collaboration.





🤔Why This Project Matters.🤔

Space exploration isn’t just about reaching new worlds. It’s about new discoveries, new experiments and the knowledge we gain from it. The equipment left behind on the Moon and Mars represent human curiosity, our desire for growth and in one way or another, our pride. By documenting these artifacts and sharing their stories, we hope to make space exploration more accessible and preserve the parts of stories of missions that are often underlooked. 

Through this project, we hope to inspire the next generation of space explorers and in one form or another, be able to help people understand the legacy of robotic exploration and the importance it holds in our society and mankind as a whole. We hope our project helps encourage curiosity about planetary science and engineering and help the future generations to come.

In the end, the mission may be over, but the story is not.
