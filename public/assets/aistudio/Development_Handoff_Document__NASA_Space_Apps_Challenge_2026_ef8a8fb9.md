### Development Handoff Document: NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 \#\#\#\# "Abandoned but not Forgotten: Storytelling about NASA's Discarded Equipment on the Moon and Mars" **Project Team** : The Everestians **Document Purpose** : Master Technical & Content Reference for AI Web Developers (Google AI Studio / React / Three.js) \#\#\#\# 1\. Project Overview \#\#\#\#\# 1.1 Mission Statement & Vision Human exploration of the solar system has transformed extraterrestrial worlds into permanent archives of technological history. From the airless basaltic plains of the Moon to the dusty basins of Mars, the asteroid belt, and interstellar drift paths, non-functional spacecraft, landing stages, rovers, and scientific payloads remain as physical monuments to human ambition. The digital museum project **"Abandoned but not Forgotten"** transforms raw planetary data and mission telemetry into an immersive, educational online experience. Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, the application allows visitors to explore off-world artifacts in 3D, trace historic mission timelines, analyze engineering specifications, and understand why these pioneering machines were left behind. \#\#\#\#\# 1.2 Target Audience & Architectural Scope \* **Primary Audience** : Students, space enthusiasts, educators, and the general public seeking accessible yet scientifically rigorous insights into space hardware heritage. \* **Developer Audience** : AI front-end developers, UI/UX designers, and 3D web graphics engineers using Google AI Studio, React, Tailwind CSS, and Three.js / React Three Fiber. \* **Technical Scope** : Standalone, interactive web application featuring interactive 3D model viewers (.glb / .usdz), global 3D planetary mapping, annotated component hotspots, side-by-side artifact comparison tools, and structured mission timelines. \#\#\#\#\# 1.3 Museum Artifact Classification System To maintain scientific rigor, all exhibits are classified under a four-tier operational disposition taxonomy: 1\. **Intentionally Left Behind Surface Infrastructure** : Equipment engineered specifically to serve as permanent surface bases, landing platforms, or passive long-term scientific stations after human or robotic departure (e.g., Apollo Lunar Module Descent Stages, ALSEP scientific arrays, Lunar Roving Vehicles). 2\. **Depleted / Environmentally Decommissioned Hardware** : Active surface explorers that completed their primary scientific missions but were silenced by environmental factors such as solar panel dust accumulation, polar winter darkness, or battery depletion (e.g., InSight Lander, Spirit & Opportunity Rovers, Phoenix Lander, Viking 1 & 2). 3\. **Hardware Malfunction & Mechanical Relics** : Spacecraft that suffered structural, mechanical, or aerodynamic injury during touchdown or operational maneuvers, rendering them stationary surface relics despite maintaining structural integrity (e.g., Ingenuity Mars Helicopter rotor blade fracture, Surveyor 2 and 4 impact sites). 4\. **Orbital, Deep Space & Small-Body Relics** : Spacecraft residing in permanent non-surface orbital paths, heliocentric drift trajectories, small-body landing sites, or outward interstellar escape vectors after exhausting onboard attitude-control propellants (e.g., Dawn Spacecraft in Ceres orbit, NEAR Shoemaker on asteroid 433 Eros, Kepler Space Telescope in Earth-trailing orbit, Pioneer 10 & 11 in interstellar space). \#\#\#\# 2\. Detailed Exhibit Database \#\#\#\#\# 3.1 Lunar Exhibits \#\#\#\#\#\# Exhibit 1: Apollo Lunar Module Descent Stages \* **Official Name** : Apollo Lunar Module (LM) Descent Stage (Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17). \* **Associated Mission** : NASA Apollo Program (1969–1972). \* **Original Purpose** : Provided controlled powered descent from lunar orbit to the surface, housing the throttleable descent engine, landing gear, propellant tanks, and surface EVA equipment. Served as the crew's operational base on the Moon. \* **Landing Locations & Coordinates** : \* Apollo 11 ( *Eagle* ): Statio Tranquillitatis — 0.67408° N, 23.47297° E. \* Apollo 12 ( *Intrepid* ): Oceanus Procellarum — 3.01239° S, 23.41863° W. \* Apollo 14 ( *Antares* ): Fra Mauro Highlands — 3.64530° S, 17.47136° W. \* Apollo 15 ( *Falcon* ): Hadley-Apennine — 26.13222° N, 3.63386° E. \* Apollo 16 ( *Orion* ): Descartes Highlands — 8.97301° S, 15.50019° E. \* Apollo 17 ( *Challenger* ): Taurus-Littrow — 20.19080° N, 30.77168° E. \* **Key Dates** : First Landing: July 20, 1969 (Apollo 11); Final Landing: December 11, 1972 (Apollo 17). \* **Technical Specifications** : \* Dry Mass: \~2,034 kg; Fueled Mass: \~10,334 kg. \* Height: 3.22 m (with legs extended); Diagonal Span: 9.4 m across touchdown pads. \* Engine: TRW Aerojet TR-201 throttleable descent engine (43.9 kN thrust). \* Structure: Aluminum alloy structure wrapped in gold and amber Kapton thermal insulation foil. \* **Reason Left Behind** : Intentionally used as the launch platform for the LM Ascent Stage. Payload return constraints required jettisoning the descent stage to allow the ascent stage to reach lunar orbit. \* **Current Status** : All six descent stages remain standing intact at their landing sites. LROC orbital photography confirms they are surrounded by astronaut tracks, equipment bags, and parked rovers. \* **Scientific Achievements & Trivia** : Enabled six successful crewed landings; housed the MESA camera equipment that broadcasted Neil Armstrong's first steps live to Earth. \* **Media & 3D Model Assets** : \* Official NASA 3D Model: Apollo Lunar Module.glb (716.8 KB, hosted on NASA 3D Resources). \#\#\#\#\#\# Exhibit 2: Lunar Roving Vehicles (LRVs) \* **Official Name** : Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV-1, LRV-2, LRV-3). \* **Associated Mission** : Apollo 15, Apollo 16, and Apollo 17 "J-series" Missions (1971–1972). \* **Original Purpose** : Electric four-wheeled mobility buggies built to transport two astronauts, science instruments, tools, and regolith samples across extended distances up to 7.5 km from the LM. \* **Landing Locations & Coordinates** : \* LRV-1 (Apollo 15): Hadley-Apennine — Parked at 26.1322° N, 3.6338° E (\~100 m from LM). \* LRV-2 (Apollo 16): Descartes Highlands — Parked at 8.9730° S, 15.5002° E (\~90 m from LM). \* LRV-3 (Apollo 17): Taurus-Littrow — Parked at 20.1908° N, 30.7717° E (\~140 m from LM, pointed at LM liftoff path). \* **Key Dates** : First Drive: July 31, 1971 (Apollo 15); Final Drive: December 13, 1972 (Apollo 17). \* **Technical Specifications** : \* Mass: 210 kg (empty mass on Earth); Payload Capacity: 490 kg (including two astronauts). \* Dimensions: 3.1 m length × 1.8 m width × 1.14 m height; Wheelbase: 2.3 m. \* Motors: Four 0.25 hp (190 W) DC electric motors (one per wheel); Power: Dual 36V silver-zinc potassium hydroxide non-rechargeable batteries. \* Tires: Woven zinc-coated steel mesh with titanium chevron treads. \* **Reason Left Behind** : Mass constraints prohibited bringing 210 kg vehicles back inside the Command Module. Vehicles were parked at designated distances to record LM ascent liftoff with their high-gain TV cameras. \* **Current Status** : Permanently parked on the lunar surface. Electrical systems are dead, but chassis structures remain fully intact. \* **Scientific Achievements & Trivia** : Allowed Apollo 17 crew to cover 35.74 km of total driving distance; reached top speed of 18 km/h driven by Gene Cernan. \* **Media & 3D Model Assets** : \* High-resolution NASA surface photos available (AS15-88-11901). No standalone official NASA GLB scan currently hosted on NASA 3D Resources. \#\#\#\#\#\# Exhibit 3: ALSEP Packages & Laser Retroreflectors \* **Official Name** : Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package (ALSEP) & Laser Ranging Retroreflector (LRRR). \* **Associated Mission** : Apollo 11 (EASEP), Apollo 12, 14, 15, 16, 17 (1969–1972). \* **Original Purpose** : Autonomous instrument arrays deployed on the surface to measure moonquakes, solar wind, magnetic fields, heat flow, and lunar atmospheric density over multi-year periods. \* **Locations** : Deployed within 100–300 meters of each Apollo landing site. \* **Key Dates** : Deployed 1969–1972; Official NASA telemetry shutdown: September 30, 1977\. \* **Technical Specifications** : \* Power: SNAP-27 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator (RTG) producing \~70W DC power fueled by Plutonium-238. \* Primary Instruments: Passive Seismic Experiment (PSE), Lunar Surface Magnetometer (LSM), Heat Flow Experiment (HFE). \* LRRR Design: Array of 100 fused silica corner-cube optical prisms (Apollo 11 & 14: 100 prisms; Apollo 15: 300 prisms). \* **Reason Left Behind & Current Status** : Engineered as permanent surface installations.
